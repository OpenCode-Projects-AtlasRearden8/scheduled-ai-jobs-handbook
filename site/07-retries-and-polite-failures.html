07 · Retries and polite failures

Networks hiccup. Ollama reboots. A model server takes forty seconds to load a big model and your job timed out at twenty. None of that means "give up on today's digest" — it means try again, gently, then fail with dignity.

The retry sandwich

When ai_job.py calls a flaky endpoint, here's what actually happens:

responses — sleep 2s, then 4s, then 8s (doubling, capped at 30s). error: ...` line, leave exit code 1.

  1. Try — one POST to /chat/completions with your timeout attached.
  2. If it fails — disconnects, timeouts, HTTP 500-class errors, empty
  3. After N attempts — give up, log one clear `FAILED ... model call
python3 scripts/ai_job.py \
  --base-url http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1 \
  --prompt "Digest today's notes" \
  --input-file my-notes.md \
  --retries 3 --timeout 120

Defaults: --retries 3 attempts, --timeout 120 seconds each. Backoff starts at 2 seconds.

Timeouts: the quarantine for slow endpoints

The model didn't fail; the model is loading. Either way, at 7:05 you care less about why than when it stops. --timeout sets the per-request deadline: bigger models — the 70B-class local models — are slow. Having a timeout is why hung is a state your job exits from, not a state it lives in.

The alerting question

Polite failure quietly backstops one gap: nobody's watching at 7 AM. The generous rule is a building rule:

(Chapter 9) runs on a schedule, summarizes fails from logs' recent window, and exits 2 so your wrapper script can say "email me" or "notification."

  • First level: the humble log. Check it at your weekly review.
  • Second level: make the failure surface once a week — the watchdog

A notify-if-failed.cmd pattern:

cd /d C:\scheduled-ai-jobs
C:\Python313\python.exe scripts\watchdog.py --job-dir logs --failure-window-hours 24
if errorlevel 2 echo AI jobs had findings on %date% >> logs\alerter.log

(Who gets emailed is your infrastructure; until you wire a mailer, "you watching the logs" is the alert — which is fine if the watchdog runs on a schedule and its findings land somewhere you look weekly.)

The one test that matters

tests/run_tests.py exercises both sides: a mock endpoint that fails the first 2 requests (MOCK_FAIL_FIRST=2) → the job succeeds on the 3rd try; a persistently failing one (MOCK_FAIL=1) → the job logs one FAILED line and exits 1. If you wrap ai_job.py with your own retry or alerting layer, write those two tests first. They're the whole contract.
